Recorded native C runs, original and corrected source

The Borg shot at a sleeping troll because its melee danger came out at 5%

Before it attacks a sleeping monster from a distance, the Borg asks how dangerous that monster would be if it woke, and leaves hard sleeping monsters alone. That estimate scales a monster's melee damage by the chance that its blows hit. The chance was computed as 150 minus the armour term armour*75 minus 3*(level+power) where it should divide the armour term by the monster's to-hit. Any armour class of 2 or more therefore gave the 5% floor, and a higher monster level lowered the chance. A sleeping cave troll counted as harmless and the Borg shot at it. The correction divides armour by the to-hit, so the chance falls as armour rises and rises with the monster's level, and caps the chance at 100% so the partial estimate never exceeds the full damage of a blow; the check works.

A level-15 Warrior at full hit points (177) with 10 armour sees a sleeping cave troll six grids east. The production ranged-attack scorer rates a bolt at it. Original: the partial melee danger of the troll is 0 (a 5% hit chance), so the check that avoids waking a hard sleeping monster does not trigger and the bolt scores 12. Corrected: every blow's hit chance reaches the 100% cap, so the partial melee danger is 162, equal to the full-damage danger and well above half the Borg's avoidance (88), and the scorer refuses the attack. In both runs the partial estimate does not exceed the full-damage figure.

Experiment conditions and limits

Native Human Warrior, seed 63, character level 15, 177 hit points, 10 armour, depth 5, a lit open arena, a lit torch. The production borg_launch_bolt() scores a bolt of damage 12 (about a Magic Missile) at the monster; the fixture builds the target list for the one monster in view and sets the attacking flag exactly as borg_attack() does, and the monster is marked asleep in the game and in the Borg's monster list. Nothing is fired or played. All partial-damage callers share the corrected estimate; only this one decision is measured.
