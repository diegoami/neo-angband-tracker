The Borg counts the throwing giant as an obstacle to its own boulder

A Rogue sees a hill giant six squares away, with no creature or wall between them. The original path check stops on the giant itself and rates its boulder at zero danger. A native scheduled boulder still hits for 18 HP. The correction recognizes the clear shot; it then spends 2 mana teleporting but remains exposed and takes 29 HP in this recording.

This fixes a false blocked-shot assessment. The unfavorable hill-giant outcome remains visible; no uniform safety improvement is claimed. The clear-path storm-giant example shows another consequence: the correction uses a scroll, moves behind a wall and avoids the scheduled spell. Actual intervening creatures and walls remain obstacles.

Experiment conditions and limits

Native Rogue level 20, 130 HP/121 mana, depth 38, seed 1000, known Phase Door spell and one scroll, constructed Intelligence/arena setup. The real stock monster spell lists keep their native order. The helper-only source changes one obstruction check, retaining the separate mixed-spell classification defect.

Production danger and escape routines choose. Actual spell/book or scroll keys are validated and executed natively. One scheduled valid native spell effect follows only with actual range and terrain access; the real active-monster context supplies spell power. This does not run monster AI spell selection/frequency/failure or all player fallback/world turns.

Native damage, saving throws, spell/scroll cost and movement are not substituted. Different selected actions naturally consume different random draws; no RNG is reset. Danger estimates are heuristics, not HP predictions. The shown Phase Door spells succeed despite a native 5% failure chance. No extra enemy action, death, encounter frequency or overall survival claim.
