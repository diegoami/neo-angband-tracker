One bad neighbour stopped the Borg from backing away

When a monster threatens a hurt Borg, it can step back to a safer neighbouring grid. It checks the eight neighbours in a fixed order, starting with the one to the south. Two slips cut that check short: once one candidate grid was next to a monster, every later candidate was also treated as next to a monster; and a known trap on one candidate ended the whole check. A level-20 Warrior on 40 hit points, with a cave bear two steps away, therefore did not back away even though the grid to the north was safe. The correction judges each grid on its own, and the Borg backs up to the north-west.

Original: after the south grid is found next to the bear, every later grid is also skipped; no retreat. Corrected: '# Backing up to 9,11', a step north-west (key 55).

Experiment conditions and limits

Native Human Warrior, seed 155, level 20, depth 5, starting kit, a lit room, no staircases. An awake cave bear two steps from the Borg (danger estimate 20 at the Borg's grid; the back-away step needs danger above 40% of the hit points). The Borg has been on the level 1000 game turns and no anti-summon corridor is in use. 'monster': bear south-south-east, so the first grid checked (south) is next to it. 'trap': bear south-south-west and a known pit on the south grid (placed after the bear so the bear's roll is identical). 'healthy' control: as 'monster' but at full hit points (245), below the back-away threshold. The production borg_caution() runs once and the first queued key is read back. This isolates the retreat decision.

Canonical report: issue 155.
