Recorded native item choice, potion use and map knowledge

The Borg recognizes Enlightenment but never drinks it

A Warrior carries a known Enlightenment potion in the dungeon, with stairs beyond a wall still unknown. The original useful-item routine leaves the potion unused and those stairs unknown. After the correction it drinks one potion, and the native game reveals the stairs.

Enlightenment reveals the level's layout. This is a utility fix: no faster escape, avoided attack, injury or death is measured. The existing rule against drinking it in town remains.

Why the correction works

The potion is recognized and its town restriction checked, but the original branch never requests a drink. The correction adds the missing drink inside that branch. Five complete control logs stay identical: town, unknown flavor, unrelated potion, and Augmentation in town and dungeon.

Experiment conditions and limits

Native Human Warrior level 40, depth 30 (town controls use depth 0), seed 1000, light, food and a quiet constructed arena. A wall blocks the stair ten squares to the east, with a passage around its end. Native line of sight and player knowledge confirm that the stair is unknown. The actual Borg safety check accepts the location.

The test calls the real useful-item routine, validates the exact queued potion, then native potion use computes its effect and reveals the stair in the player's map memory. No map knowledge, replacement potion or successful effect is injected.

This isolates the utility action, not the whole exploration/caution loop. The town controls use the same constructed arena at depth 0, not a generated town/store visit. No monster turns, route choice, travel time or survival improvement are tested. The potion cost is real: the correction consumes one item to obtain that information.
