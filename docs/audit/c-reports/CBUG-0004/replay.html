Acid protection should not count as cold protection

A Rogue protected from acid stands beside a snow troll with cold bites. The original Borg stays and loses 21 HP in the scheduled attack. The correction recognizes that cold protection is missing, casts Phase Door and avoids that attack, spending 2 mana.

The reverse case matters too: with cold protection alone, the correction stays and saves 2 mana but takes 20 HP. The change is not uniformly better for HP. Both results are shown below.

What the test measures

The real Borg chooses whether to escape. The test executes its actual spell or scroll command through the native game. It then schedules one normal monster attack only if the monster remains adjacent. The real game determines hits and damage.

This isolates the escape decision. It does not run the rest of the Borg's turn, monster pursuit, other attacks or a complete fight. It does not establish how often this situation occurs, that the Borg would choose the same later action in full play, a death, or an overall survival improvement. Both versions use unchanged setup, seed and assertions.

Phase Door is a short teleport. Casting it can fail; the Rogue's native failure chance here is 5%. The successful escape shown is the actual fixed-seed result, not a guaranteed outcome for every game.

Why the correction changes these decisions

The Borg mistakenly applies acid protection when estimating a cold melee attack. The correction applies cold protection instead. In the acid-only recording the estimated danger changes from 52 to116, crossing the existing escape threshold104. With cold protection alone it changes from116 to52. These are decision scores, not measured HP damage. The HP values above come from the separate native attack.

Cold protection does not eliminate the physical part of a bite or the troll's claws. That is why the protected character can still lose20 HP after choosing to stay. No damage rule or escape threshold was changed.
