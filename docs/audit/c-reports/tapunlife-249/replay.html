Recorded native Tap Unlife decisions

Tap Unlife was rated at the target's full hit points

The Borg's Tap Unlife case computes its mana gain as current mana minus current mana — always zero — so the spell's rating falls through to the monster's full estimated hp. Any undead therefore looks like a one-shot worth finishing, and the borg prefers a full-health undead over one it could actually finish with the same free cast. The correction rates the tap at its real damage, like the other dispel cases, and ignores the mana gain as the code comment already says.

What the controls establish

With only the plain giant, both versions cast the bolt; with only the small bat, both versions cast the tap. Those two complete logs match byte for byte, so the correction changes nothing except the huge-undead case.

Experiment conditions and limits

Native Human Necromancer level 14, depth 30, seed 1000, glowing constructed arena, both shadow books ([Into the Shadows], [Dark Rituals]), Nether Bolt and Tap Unlife learned, full mana and hp, no light source (a necromancer's fail rate rises by 25 on a lit square). The production attack chooser acts on its own filled target list; the cast is shown by its queued keys, not executed. This isolates the tap rating, not full autonomous play; no damage or survival claim.
