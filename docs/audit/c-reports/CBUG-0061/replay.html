The Borg hesitates to drink a healing potion after the wrong delay

A wounded, confused Warrior has seven Cure Critical Wounds potions and last drank one five Borg turns ago. The original leaves them unused, stays at 321 HP and remains confused. The correction drinks one potion, heals to 381 HP and clears confusion.

The correction conserves a potion just after a recent drink instead. That leaves the recent-drink example wounded and confused on this call: a resource tradeoff, not an overall survival improvement.




Original

Why a simple reversal is insufficient

The original passes 5 of 9 cases; reversing the comparison passes 8. That simple change blocks the first-ever drink at internal turn 1: zero is the stored value for no previous drink, not a real previous drink. Checking that a previous drink exists passes all 9 cases and preserves five complete control logs.

Experiment conditions and limits

Native Human Warrior level 40, depth 30, seed 1000, known potions. The test establishes a real earlier drink and applies controlled injuries/confusion. It advances the Borg's internal decision clock directly to isolate the elapsed-time check; it does not simulate intervening world turns or combat. Every source version uses the same setup and seed, with no RNG reset between decisions. The native potion handler computes the healing and cure and removes the actual potion.

This tests the shared potion-use helper, not the entire healing/escape decision loop. Emergency callers can bypass the hesitation rule; that path is tested unchanged. It does not prove an extra enemy attack, death, encounter frequency or that conserving a potion is always safer. The random hesitation remains probabilistic, and the result shown is one unchanged seeded experiment.
