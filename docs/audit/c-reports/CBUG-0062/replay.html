A charged fire ring is overlooked on the left hand

A Warrior has a charged Ring of Flames on the left hand and an ogre in range. The original ring-attack check requests no activation, leaving the ogre at70 HP. The complete correction activates the left ring and kills the ogre, removing its70 HP.

A Ring of Flames launches a fireball and gives temporary fire protection. The right-hand ring already works; the missing left-hand ring is the problem. This test isolates the ring attack, not the whole Borg's choice among all possible attacks.

What the experiment does and does not establish

The test creates a Warrior, known rings and one visible ogre six squares away. It calls the real Borg fire-ring attack routine and validates its requested activation item and target. The native game executes that item activation, computes device success, applies the fireball, kills the monster and starts ring recharge/fire protection. No damage or success result is invented.

This is an isolated ring-attack check. It does not run the full Borg attack ranking, other available attacks, monster turns or a complete fight. The result demonstrates the missing left-ring attack and its execution after correction, not a player death or an overall survival benefit. The native device failure chance in this setup is49/1000; the shown seeded activation succeeds, not every possible attempt.

HP removed is capped at the ogre's remaining70 HP. This display does not claim that70 is the uncapped fireball damage.
